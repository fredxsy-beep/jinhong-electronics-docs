# NORD-LOCK Wedge-Locking Washers — Selection Guide | Sichuan Jinhong Electronics

Nord-Lock (Sweden, Nord-Lock Group) is the originator of wedge-locking technology. Each washer pair consists of two washers: one face carries cams, the other radial teeth. On tightening, the teeth embed into the mating surfaces; when vibration tries to loosen the bolt, the wedge effect between the cam faces (the cam angle is greater than the thread pitch angle) locks the joint tighter and tighter — mechanical locking instead of friction-based locking, verified by the Junker vibration test. ## Product Lines Overview

The wedge-locking washer range is organised into five lines by "material environment × function". First, the **original steel NL / NLsp** with zinc-flake coating — the standard stocked line for general applications; "sp" is the enlarged version (larger outside diameter for large holes, slotted holes or soft flange faces). Second, **stainless steel NLss / NLspss**, commonly in grade EN 1.4404, for damp and general corrosive environments. Third, the **254 SMO high-corrosion-resistance line NLss-254 / NLspss-254**, for chloride-bearing and other strongly corrosive environments. Fourth, **NLSC for steel construction**, for structural steel joints. Fifth, the **X-series NLX / NLXsp**, which adds a spring effect on top of wedge locking to compensate for preload loss caused by settlement and relaxation — suited to painted surfaces, soft materials, multi-layer clamping and short grip lengths, where ordinary washers tend to "embed and lose preload". No special tools are needed for installation; washers are used in pairs and pairs must not be split or mixed.

## Selection Guide

1. **Size by bolt specification first.** The number in the washer designation corresponds to the nominal bolt diameter (NL12 for M12, NL8 for M8), used one pair per joint. Where the clamped parts have large or slotted holes, or a soft mating surface, choose the enlarged sp version: the larger outside diameter spreads the surface pressure and prevents embedment.
2. **Grade the material by corrosion environment.** Dry indoor conditions: steel NL. Damp and general outdoor use: stainless NLss (EN 1.4404). Chloride-bearing environments (marine, chemical plant): step up to the 254 SMO line. If the material grade is chosen too low, the washer corrodes and fails before the bolt does.
3. **Go straight to the X-series where settlement/relaxation is a risk.** Painted or powder-coated surfaces, soft materials such as copper and aluminium, gasketed joints, short grip lengths and multi-layer clamping all settle after tightening, and ordinary washers lose preload with them; the X-series conical spring structure compensates for that loss while retaining wedge locking.
4. **Note where the X-series does not apply.** It is not recommended where the mating surface is not locked, where the mating surface is harder than the washer, or on very soft mating materials such as wood or plastic; in those cases return to the original NL series or revise the joint design.
5. **Select critical joints by the consequence of locking failure.** For high-vibration joints where failure consequences are severe — wind power, rail transit, heavy machinery, bridges — prefer wedge locking and keep the Junker test verification on record; ordinary static joints do not need to be over-specified.
6. **Reuse is conditional.** After removal, washers may be reused if the cam and tooth faces show no damage, but for safety-critical positions new washers are recommended. Genuine washers carry laser marking (traceable batch number and type code); do not use unmarked or illegibly marked washers in critical joints.

## FAQ

**Q: What is the real difference between NORD-LOCK washers and spring washers?**
A: A spring washer relies on spring force and friction; under vibration, once transverse slip occurs in the friction pair it fails quickly. NORD-LOCK relies on the geometric locking of the wedge cams — the more the bolt tries to loosen, the greater the wedging force the cam faces generate, independent of friction — and the Junker vibration test has proven its locking performance to be far superior to spring washers. For high vibration and critical positions, choose wedge locking.

**Q: How do I choose between NL and NLX (X-series)?**
A: Both share the same locking principle; the difference is that the X-series adds settlement compensation. If the joint contains factors that "embed" — coatings, soft materials, multi-layer clamping, short grip length — choose NLX. For a standard clean metal-to-metal joint with adequate grip length, NL is sufficient and costs less.

**Q: How can I tell genuine NORD-LOCK washers?**
A: Genuine washers are precisely machined, with regular cam and tooth faces, and X-series washers carry laser marking (traceable batch number and type code). Buy through regular dealer channels and avoid look-alike substitutes — locking elements usually fail without warning, so do not gamble a critical joint on washers of unknown origin.
