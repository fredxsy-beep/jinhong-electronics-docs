## Product Lines Overview

ERSA hand soldering falls into four tiers by bench grade. First, the **compact** i-CON NANO MK2 / PICO MK2: small footprint and fast to deploy, suited to single benches and laboratories. Second, the **industrial** i-CON 1 / 1V / 2V MK2, designed for the higher working temperatures and narrower process windows of lead-free processes, with the 2V as a dual-channel soldering/desoldering station. Third, the **multi-channel stations** i-CON VARIO 2 / VARIO 4, where a single main unit runs several tools at once — soldering iron, hot air, desoldering tweezers — with intelligent power distribution. Fourth, the **IoT soldering station** i-CON TRACE, which automatically records the board, component, tip, temperature and duration of every solder joint, and is the traceability solution for hand soldering. The rework line stands on its own: the HR 550 high-performance rework system and the HR 600/2 hybrid-heating automatic rework system follow an infrared-plus-convection hybrid heating and automatic placement route. Heating elements and tips can be replaced separately, which is one detail of how ERSA keeps consumable costs under control.

## Selection Guide

1. **Choose by bench grade, not by "power rating".** For everyday soldering at a single bench, the compact footprint of the NANO MK2 / PICO MK2 fits better; for fixed production-line benches choose the i-CON 1/2V MK2; where two tools must be online at the same time (soldering iron plus desoldering tool), go directly to the 2V or the VARIO series instead of buying two single units that occupy bench space.
2. **Where traceability is required, the i-CON TRACE is the dividing line.** Production lines in automotive electronics, aerospace and similar fields that require soldering process records cannot provide after-the-fact evidence with ordinary stations; the TRACE archives the parameters of every solder joint automatically and can verify before work starts that the tip and material match, reporting an error immediately if the wrong tip is fitted.
3. **For multi-channel stations, look at the "tool combination", not the channel count itself.** The value of the VARIO series lies in a soldering iron, hot air, desoldering tweezers and a desoldering iron coexisting without machine changes, which benefits benches with varied rework tasks the most; a bench with a single type of task need not pay for channel count.
4. **Grade rework systems by automation level.** Where rework is mainly manual with the occasional difficult board, the HR 550 is sufficient; for batch rework requiring placement consistency and process records, choose the HR 600/2 with its automatic alignment and placement and reflow process camera, and look at the VOIDLESS version for BGA processes sensitive to voiding.
5. **Look at the structure of the consumables bill.** ERSA heating elements and tips can be replaced separately, so whichever wears out is the one replaced; when selecting, match the tip series (102/142 and so on) to the handpiece in use — stocking tips across series is stocking the wrong tips.
6. **Plan whole-bench interlocking in advance.** Industrial i-CON models and above carry a fume-extraction interlock interface, and the VARIO can interlock with an infrared heating plate; plan extraction and preheating when the bench is built, as retrofitting later costs more.

## FAQ

**Q: What is the difference between the i-CON 1, 2V and VARIO 4?**
A: The i-CON 1/1V is a single-channel industrial soldering station, the 2V is a dual-channel soldering/desoldering station, and the VARIO 4 is the flagship station with up to four tools online at the same time. The difference is not soldering quality, but how many types of tool one bench can carry at once and whether hot air and desoldering tweezers must coexist.

**Q: Can an ordinary soldering station keep soldering records? How does ERSA do it?**
A: An ordinary station can only display the set temperature; there is no record of what was soldered, which tip was used, or what the actual temperature was. The i-CON TRACE, through networking and code scanning, automatically archives the board, component, tip, temperature and duration joint by joint, with parameters issued centrally, which suits production lines with process traceability requirements.

**Q: What does "hybrid heating" mean on the HR 600/2?**
A: The bottom uses large-area infrared to preheat the whole board while the top uses an infrared/convection hybrid heating head on the target component; the two stages work together to reduce board temperature differences and thermal stress. Combined with automatic placement by vacuum nozzle and the optional reflow process camera, it suits BGA rework on large, high-thermal-mass boards better than pure hot-air rework.
