## Product Lines Overview

The IDEAL electrical range falls into six blocks. First, **wire connectors**: twist-on (Twister®), push-in (Push-In), lever (Lever) and in-line splice (SpliceLine®) — four connection methods that form its signature product family. Second, **stripping and cutting tools**: from Stripmaster® precision wire strippers and stainless-steel strippers to benchtop and automatic stripping equipment, covering manual through to batch work. Third, **crimping and combination tools**: crimp tools, coaxial strip-and-crimp tools, and combined strip-and-crimp tools. Fourth, **general hand tools** such as pliers and cable cutters. Fifth, **wire-processing equipment**, such as the Wire Master™ laser wire stripper, Stripmaster® automatic wire strippers and the Ringer® shielded-wire jacket stripper, for harness processing and aerospace cable work. Sixth, **test and measurement, safety products (Lockout/Tagout and 1000V insulated tools) and tool storage**. The items Jinhong lists in its store are mainly stripping tools.

## Selection Guide

1. **Match the stripper blade to the wire gauge**: Stripmaster-type precision strippers have blades graded by AWG (45-090 for 8–12 AWG, 45-096 for 6–8 AWG, Lite for 16–22 AWG). The wrong grade either fails to strip or nicks the conductor. Count the gauge ranges most used at the station first, then fix the model combination.
2. **Check solid and stranded wire separately**: the same stainless-steel stripper covers different gauges for solid and stranded wire (for example, 45-220 is 10–18 AWG solid / 12–20 AWG stranded). Check against the actual wire type, not a single figure.
3. **Use a dedicated stripper for shielded-wire jackets**: when stripping the outer jacket of shielded cable, use a dedicated tool such as the Ringer to ring-cut and remove the jacket without the blade scoring the shield braid; ordinary strippers and hand knives have a high rework rate on shielded cable.
4. **Grade connectors by wire range and voltage**: in the Twister PRO family, Mini handles small gauges, Plus covers the usual #22–#8 AWG range, and Max is rated to 1000V and handles up to #6 AWG. For distribution work with many branches and mixed gauges, prefer Plus / Max rather than forcing large wire into a small grade.
5. **Upgrade to equipment for batch work**: where manual stripping cannot keep takt time, or the cable has special insulation, upgrade to an automatic stripper or a laser wire stripper (Wire Master) and let the equipment control strip length and consistency. Precision cable and fibre have dedicated strippers such as the 45-350; do not mix them with electrical wire stripping tools.
6. **Kit out live and safety work as a set**: for stations involving distribution maintenance, list insulated tools (1000V) and Lockout/Tagout products together with the hand tools when selecting.

## FAQ

**Q: What is the difference between a Stripmaster precision stripper and an ordinary wire stripper?**
A: An ordinary stripper relies on feel to control the jaw opening, so it easily nicks the conductor or fails to strip. The Stripmaster uses formed blades matched to the wire gauge and strips in one squeeze, giving consistent strip lengths without damaging the conductor — suited to harness processing and other work where consistency matters. The trade-off is that the model grade must be matched to the wire gauge.

**Q: What does the Ringer shielded-wire jacket stripper do?**
A: It is dedicated to removing the outer jacket of shielded cable: the ring-cut depth is controlled so it severs only the jacket, without damaging the shield braid or conductors inside. Jacket treatment on aerospace harnesses and shielded signal cables is its typical application, and it is one of the IDEAL items listed by Jinhong.
