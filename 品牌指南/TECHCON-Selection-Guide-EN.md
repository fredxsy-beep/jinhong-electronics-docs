## Product Lines Overview

TECHCON is organised in five tiers by automation level. First, **digital dispensers** (TS250/TS255 and similar): time/pressure control, suited to benchtop manual and semi-automatic dispensing. Second, **time/pressure dispensing controllers** (TS350/TS355, TS500R and similar): precision pressure regulation plus suck-back, with dispensed-volume repeatability reaching the ±1% class; these are the mainstay of SMT dispensing, chip underfill and similar processes. Third, the **valve family**: diaphragm valves, needle valves, spool valves, spray valves, auger (rotary screw) valves and jet valves, divided by adhesive viscosity and dispensing method. Fourth, **smart valve controllers** (TS555R/TS560R/TS580R/TS980): touchscreen plus networking, with parameters remotely monitored, aimed at Industry 4.0 production lines. Fifth, **benchtop dispensing robots and jetting systems** (the TSR robot series and the TS9800 jetting system), completing the upgrade path from manual work to full automation. On the consumables side there are syringes, needles and dual-component cartridge systems; matching consumables from the same brand as the machine avoids compatibility trial and error.

## Selection Guide

1. **Choose the valve by adhesive viscosity first, then the controller** (per TECHCON's official white-paper guidance): pinch valves suit 1–45 kcps, needle valves 1–100 kcps, diaphragm valves 1–50 kcps, spool valves 5k–3,000 kcps, rotary auger valves 30k–1,300 kcps, and spray valves 1–10 kcps. If the viscosity does not match, no controller, however expensive, can rescue the process.
2. **For stringing, check the "shut-off" capability first.** Stringing dots and inconsistent dot sizes mostly come from control rather than from the adhesive itself: a controller with suck-back pulls the adhesive at the needle tip back at the instant dispensing ends, leaving a clean finish. Underfill processes in particular must take shut-off seriously — strings that trap bubbles form voids and undermine BGA reinforcement reliability.
3. **Do the accuracy maths for manual dispensing.** Direct-pressure manual syringe dispensing can vary by as much as ±15%, while a time/pressure controller can bring dispensed-volume repeatability to the ±1% class and control dot diameter down to 0.2 mm. With costly materials such as photoresist and silver paste, consistency translates directly into material savings.
4. **Contact dispensing or non-contact jetting?** Where the workpiece surface is uneven, Z-axis collision is a concern, or takt time is demanding, choose the TS9800/TS9200D jetting solutions; for ordinary flat dispensing, a valve plus controller is more economical. When selecting a jet valve, first confirm the adhesive viscosity ceiling and the minimum dot requirement.
5. **Changeover speed is a hidden benefit.** On multi-product small-batch lines, choose a programmable controller — program switching takes under 1 minute; upgrade to a TSR robot only when automatic alignment and path dispensing are needed, matching the model to the work-area size (200/300/400 mm).
6. **Do not compromise on needles and consumables.** Precision metal needles go down to a 0.102 mm inner diameter, and needle specification is directly related to dot size; choose amber syringes for UV adhesives, and prefer seal-less diaphragm valves or replaceable-fluid-path solutions for moisture-sensitive adhesives.

## FAQ

**Q: Dispensing shows stringing and inconsistent dot sizes — is it the adhesive or the equipment?**
A: In most cases it is a control problem. Air-pressure fluctuation and the absence of suck-back shut-off make the dispensed volume drift and the dot ending string. First change to a time/pressure controller with precision pressure regulation and suck-back (such as the TS500R), then re-check that the valve type matches the adhesive viscosity, and only then consider changing the adhesive.

**Q: The TS250 and TS255 differ by only one digit — which should I choose?**
A: The difference is the pressure range: the TS250 is the 0–100 psi general-purpose version, while the TS255 is the 0–15 psi low-pressure version, which adjusts more finely for micro-dispensing. Choose the TS250 for ordinary adhesives and the TS255 for low-viscosity micro-dispensing.

**Q: When should I move up to a dispensing robot or a jet valve?**
A: Upgrade when three kinds of signal appear: dot-position consistency can no longer be held by manual alignment, frequent changeovers are driving up training and trial-dispensing costs, or the workpiece must not be touched (fragile surfaces / three-dimensional structures). Choose a TSR-series robot for the first cases and the TS9800 jetting system for the last.
