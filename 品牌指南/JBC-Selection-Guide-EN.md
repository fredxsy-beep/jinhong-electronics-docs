## Product Lines Overview

JBC organises its range into four lines by bench scale and task. First, the **Compact series**: single-tool all-in-one stations including CDS (precision), CDB (general purpose), CDA (automatic solder feed), CDP (micro tweezers) and CDD (desoldering), ready to use out of the box. Second, the **Premium modular series**: built around DDE/DDU/DME/DMU control units, one unit can manage several tools at once and is compatible with up to 10 JBC tools, including Nano stations and four-tool rework stations — suited to multi-bench setups and production lines with demanding process management. Third, the **Heavy Duty series**, fitted with the T470 heavy-duty handle for large joints and high thermal-mass loads. Fourth, the **hot-air rework line**: JTSE (high power), TESE (precision) and JNA (precision / nitrogen) are divided by component size. Soldering tips form a system matched to each handle: C115 (nano), C120 (micro tweezers), C210 (precision), C245 (general purpose), C250, C470 (heavy duty) and C560 (desoldering) — more than 500 tip shapes across the range, each matched one-to-one to its handle and not interchangeable across series.

## Selection Guide

1. **Start from the bench task: soldering station vs. hot-air station.** For everyday soldering and touch-up, a soldering station is the main tool; desoldering, chip-level rework and hot-air component removal require hot-air equipment. For mixed soldering-and-rework benches, pair a soldering station with a hot-air station rather than switching one unit back and forth and slowing the work down.
2. **Single bench: Compact. Multi-bench or process-managed lines: Premium.** Compact all-in-one stations are cost-effective and fast to deploy. The value of Premium modular stations lies in dynamic soldering profiles (controlling the heating ramp to avoid thermal shock on ceramic components such as MLCCs), temperature limits, PIN parameter protection and data traceability — choose Premium where line process control requirements are high.
3. **Match handle and tip by system.** Precision small joints: T210 + C210. General soldering: T245 + C245. High thermal-mass joints: T470 + C470. Nano-level rework: NT115 + C115. Tips and handles are not interchangeable across series, so when purchasing, fix the handle from your main joint types first, then stock the corresponding tip range.
4. **Choose hot-air power by component size.** Smallest SMD (01005 to SOIC-8 class): JNA precision hot air. Small-to-medium SMD: TESE (300 W). Large devices and high thermal-mass boards: JTSE (700 W). Use extractors and protection plates to concentrate heat on the target component and protect neighbouring devices.
5. **Use sleep/hibernation to extend tip life.** JBC's intelligent thermal management drops the tip below solder melting point while the handle is idle, with hibernation delay programmable from 0–60 minutes; JBC states tip life can reach up to 5 times that of conventional use. When deploying, set the sleep/hibernation parameters to your bench rhythm — do not switch them off.
