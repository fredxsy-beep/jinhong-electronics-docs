## Product Line Overview

A MEISEI heat stripping system consists of two parts: a **power supply** and a **stripping handle**. Power supplies come in three tiers: the M-10 general-purpose unit, the M-20 ESD-safe unit, and the M-30 series for digital temperature control. The power supply does not include a handle, so the two must always be specified as a matched set. Handles fall into two families: the 4 Series (HOTWEEZERS 4A / 4B / 4C and HOTNIFE 2A) pairs with the M-10, while the 7 Series (HOTWEEZERS 7A / 7B / 7C and HOTNIFE 3A) is the ESD-safe family and pairs with the M-20. The two families use different connectors and different output voltages (approx. 6.3 V for the 4 Series, approx. 12.6 V for the 7 Series), so **power supplies and handles must never be mixed across families**. The stripping heads have an adjustable length stop, so strip lengths stay uniform across a whole batch. The power supply also features holder-triggered standby: returning the handle to its holder puts it into standby, reducing idle heating and oxidation of the stripping head. Because the insulation is softened by heat rather than cut, the stripped end is clean and square with zero nicking of the conductor — ideal for batch end preparation of fine wire (AWG 28 and finer), silver-plated wire, coaxial cable and PTFE-insulated wire.

## Selection Guide

1. **Choose the power supply first, then the stripping head.** If the workstation has ESD protection requirements, go straight to the M-20 + 7 Series; if not, choose the M-10 + 4 Series. The two families use different connectors and their power supplies are not interchangeable, so a wrong choice cannot be substituted later. If you need digital temperature control so that temperature can be quantified and recorded in process documentation, upgrade the power supply to the M-30 series.
2. **Match the stripping head to the wire gauge.** Use the A hole group for fine wire from AWG 36–24, the B hole group for the commonly used AWG 28–18 gauges, and the C flat blade for flat, ribbon and jacket stripping. Stripping fine wire in an oversized hole gives an incomplete strip, and using the wrong hole group can crush the insulation at its root. If the conductor OD exceeds approx. 6.35 mm or the cross-section is irregular, do not force the flat blade — select a HOTNIFE instead.
3. **Set the temperature by insulation material.** The manufacturer's manual gives these starting reference settings: PVC 2.5, polyethylene 4.0, nylon 5.0, silicone rubber 7.0, Teflon (PTFE) 8.0, Kynar 9.0. The principle is to stay close to the material's softening point and below its charring point. On first use, test-strip on scrap wire and fine-tune the setting, then set the length stop for batch work — never carry a setting over from a different material.
4. **One power supply can be extended with multiple handles.** Simply add handles to cover the gauges your shop strips most often, instead of one power supply per workstation. Note, however, that the two sockets of the M-10 cannot be used at the same time — swap in the handle that matches the gauge you are working on.
5. **Manage stripping heads as consumables.** Under daily use, a stripping head lasts 18–24 months or more, and many can be assessed for repair. If strip quality drops, check three things first: the temperature setting, head wear, and whether the hole group matches the wire gauge. For critical workstations, it is advisable to keep a spare handle of the same model on hand. The standard cord length is 3 ft, with 4 ft and 6 ft options available; do not extend the lead wire yourself.

## FAQ

**Q: Why doesn't a heat stripper damage the conductor when stripping PTFE wire?**
A: Thermal stripping softens the insulation with heat and then removes it in one piece; the blade never contacts the conductor during the process, leaving a clean, square end of consistent length. With mechanical strippers on PTFE wire, a blade set too deep nicks the conductor, while one set too shallow leaves insulation behind — and a nicked conductor tends to break from inside the insulation under vibration, a classic hidden failure source. For high-reliability stripping of fine wire, silver-plated wire and coaxial cable, thermal stripping is the more dependable process.

**Q: How do I choose between the M-10 and the M-20? Can a 7 Series handle be used with an M-10 power supply?**
A: The difference lies in ESD protection and the matching handle family: the M-10 is the general-purpose (non-ESD) unit and pairs with 4 Series handles, while the M-20 is ESD-safe and pairs with 7 Series handles. A 7 Series handle cannot be used with an M-10 power supply — ESD and non-ESD handles have different connectors and different output voltages (approx. 12.6 V vs. approx. 6.3 V), and the power supplies must not be mixed. The stripping principle and temperature range are the same for both families.

**Q: What causes inconsistent strip lengths or incomplete stripping?**
A: Inconsistent lengths are usually due to the length stop not being used or having worked loose — once the stop is set, a whole batch stays uniform. For incomplete stripping, check in turn whether the temperature is set for the material, whether the stripping head is worn, and whether the hole group matches the wire gauge; checking these three items will pinpoint the cause.
